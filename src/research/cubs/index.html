How do you grade a rebuild that won the championship—but did not become a dynasty?

Report 004 is a lifecycle study, not an executive ranking. It separates foundation building, player development, asset conversion, peak quality, October conversion, durability and the second build so one World Series cannot answer every question by itself.

Start here

The model follows the entire rebuild lifecycle.

1

Define the phases

Foundation 2012–14. Championship window 2015–18. Decline/last stand 2019–21. Second build 2022–25.

2

Follow the assets

Drafted stars, inherited prospects, buy-low trades, veteran additions and downstream trade chains are credited separately.

3

Separate peak from durability

A historically great 2016 team can score 100 for peak quality while the lifecycle still loses points for failing to keep regenerating the window.

4

Count traded prospects correctly

Dylan Cease, Alex Lange, DJ Herz, Jackson Ferris and Cam Smith can create trade value without being labeled Cubs-developed MLB success.

5

Try to manufacture a dynasty

Named philosophies, leave-one-out tests and 300,000 random-weight models test how hard the priorities must be bent to reach a dynasty-level score.

What the model cannot claim

Lifecycle scoring still contains judgment.

2026 is excluded

Report 004 stops after completed 2025. The active 2026 Cubs cannot improve or damage the historical score yet.

Recent drafts are immature

2022–25 classes remain tracked but excluded from the mature development average; 2021 is transitional.

2020 needs context

The 34–26 pandemic season remains in the story but is normalized when volume comparisons require it.

A traded prospect can create value without becoming a Cubs development win

This prevents hindsight from counting Cease or Cam Smith twice.

“Not a dynasty” is not “failed rebuild”

The 80.6 score explicitly classifies the lifecycle as a championship-caliber rebuild and great window.
