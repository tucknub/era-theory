How do you judge the full life of a championship window?

Report 004 is not a GM ranking and not a dynasty label. It asks whether Chicago built the 2016 core well, maximized it while the window was open, recognized the decline at the right time, converted the teardown into useful future value and actually built another contender.

Start here

The method follows the window from construction through the next contender.

1

Lock the stages first

Build 2012–14. Championship Window 2015–20. Teardown / Reset 2021–24. Re-entry 2025. The active 2026 season is excluded.

2

Separate creation from inheritance

Báez and Contreras were inherited organizational assets. Rizzo, Bryant, Hendricks, Arrieta, Schwarber and others arrived through different channels. Acquisition and development credit are not automatically the same.

3

Grade the window without ring halo

The 2016 title is rewarded primarily in postseason conversion. It does not make every free-agent contract or deadline trade a good decision.

4

Judge the exit using information available then

A 95-win 2018 team is not retroactively treated as an obvious seller. The timeline asks when the decline evidence became strong enough to justify sacrificing another run.

5

Follow the 2021 assets into 2025

The report distinguishes direct teardown descendants, retained old-window Cubs, new development, new spending and later unrelated trades.

What the model cannot claim

Lifecycle scoring still contains judgment.

2026 is excluded

Report 004 stops after completed 2025. The active 2026 Cubs cannot improve or damage the frozen historical score.

Re-entry is only one complete season

The 92-win 2025 team is real evidence, but one season is not proof of a durable new championship window.

The 2025 role-weight index is not WAR

The transparent 1–5 role weight is only a causal provenance check used to estimate where the roster came from. It does not directly enter the seven-dimension score.

Some teardown value remains immature

Kevin Alcántara and other young assets retained future value at the cutoff, so realized-return grades may change with later seasons.

2020 needs context

The 34–26 pandemic season remains in the story but is treated carefully when volume comparisons require full-season context.

Why isn't the workbook public? The native 28-tab research database remains private. Public transparency comes from the evidence inventory, model weights, source policy, limitations and robustness tests.
