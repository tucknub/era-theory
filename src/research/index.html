Published scoring model

Seven dimensions. Results matter most.

The final score is a weighted organizational model, not a win percentage and not an opinion poll. The underlying workbook keeps full-precision values; the public report displays one decimal place.

DimensionConstructionWeight
Team results70% win percentage + 30% winning-season rate25%

Stress testing

Robustness result

Polian won 97.7% of unrestricted random-weight draws, 100% of bounded draws and all seven leave-one-dimension-out tests.

Counterexamples retained

Ballard wins the deliberately narrow drafting-plus-transactions stress test. Grigson wins the roster-plus-resilience-only stress test. The system can produce another winner when the philosophy genuinely changes.

Private workbook policy

The complete workbook is intentionally not published as an unrestricted public file. Public transparency comes from publishing the methodology, formulas, evidence inventory, source families, exclusions and reproducible model behavior while protecting the organized research asset itself.

Known limitations

What the model does not pretend to know.

Public information only

No private scouting boards, medical files, ownership discussions, contract negotiations or internal coaching evaluations are available.

Recent classes withheld

The 2023–2025 draft classes are tracked but excluded from mature class scoring until enough NFL seasons exist.

Unresolved future capital

The Sauce Gardner trade is recorded but excluded from the resource index until the on-field return and future first-round opportunity cost can be judged responsibly.

2026 remains live

The final historical verdict is locked to completed seasons through 2025. Active 2026 results do not enter the published score.

Judgment is not hidden

Some draft, transaction, coaching and resilience components use evidence-coded judgment. Component rules remain explicit so readers can challenge them.

Robustness is not truth probability

The 300,000 random-weight runs show whether the ranking is sensitive to reasonable priorities; they are not a probability that one executive is objectively “correct.”
