How do we measure a franchise that keeps changing stars without starting over?

Report 003 is not an executive ranking. It follows three competitive Pacers cores and the value chain connecting them. The private workbook contains 28 tabs; the public method, scoring rules, source audit and robustness results are here.

Start here

The report follows value, not job titles.

1

Define the competitive cores

Paul George: 2010–11 through 2016–17. Oladipo/Sabonis: 2017–18 through 2021–22. Haliburton: 2022–23 through completed 2025–26.

2

Follow the star chain

Granger→George internally, George→Oladipo/Sabonis, Oladipo→LeVert/Sabonis-led phase, Sabonis→Haliburton.

3

Separate inputs from outcomes

Drafting, trades, coaching and elite talent are measured separately from regular-season and postseason results.

4

Keep injuries in the story

George, Oladipo and Haliburton injury seasons remain in results. Resilience grades the response, not the fact that an injury happened.

5

Try to break the verdict

Named philosophies, leave-one-out tests and 300,000 random-weight models show when another core can reasonably win.

Eight NBA questions

The NBA model is different on purpose.

15%

Team strength & results

Win percentage and frequency of winning seasons.

20%

Postseason ceiling & conversion

Series wins, deepest round and series-win conversion per playoff berth.

20%

Star succession & asset conversion

How well Indiana created, preserved and converted lead-star value.

10%

Draft & development

Pacers-specific mature class value; young classes are shrunk or withheld instead of prematurely crowned.

10%

Roster-resource strategy

Trades, signings, extensions, exits and retained flexibility.

10%

Coaching & system identity

Selection quality, roster fit, adaptability and governance.

10%

Elite talent & core quality

Honor density, elite overlap, playoff depth and retention through the core peak.

5%

Availability & resilience

Preparedness, replacement quality, tactical adjustment and result preservation during major shocks.

Can the formula be blamed?

Yes, priorities can change the winner—and we show that.

The counterargument is real. Paul George wins the regular-season-durability and resilience-heavy philosophies, and wins the leave-one-out test when postseason achievement is removed. This is a qualified verdict, not a landslide.

What the model cannot claim

Report 003 is deliberately unfinished where the evidence is unfinished.

The Haliburton core is still active

Its eventual exit/succession event is unobserved and receives no penalty.

2023–2025 draft classes are young

They remain visible but are excluded from mature-class averages; the 2022 class is the only mature Haliburton-era class at this cutoff.

The Zubac trade is unresolved

Its cost is recorded, including the 2026 No. 5 pick, but five Pacers games are not enough to final-grade the return.

“No long tank” is not “never lost”

Indiana had bad seasons. The supported claim is that it repeatedly carried existing value into the next core instead of rebuilding from zero for years.
