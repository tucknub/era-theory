Report 003 · Indiana Pacers · 2010–2026

How did we test the star-to-star conversion story?

This report does not rank general managers. It compares three competitive cores and asks whether Indiana repeatedly preserved enough star value to build the next one without relying on a planned multi-year bottom-out.

Show the construction behind those categories

Team Strength: 70% regular-season index + 30% elite-talent score. Regular-season index = 60% win percentage + 25% winning-season rate + 15% positive-net-rating-season rate.

Postseason: 25 points playoff-berth rate + 45 normalized points for series wins per core-season + 30-point deepest-run ladder.

Star succession: entry/creation quality 40% + internal succession/overlap 30% + exit asset preservation 30%. If an exit has not happened, applicable components are renormalized instead of assigning zero.

Draft/development: mature-class index after three completed NBA seasons. A player selected for another team in a prearranged draft-night trade receives zero Pacers development credit.

Resources: cost/leverage 30 + fit/timing 25 + value delivered 25 + asset preservation/flexibility 20.

System: continuity 20 + roster-system fit 30 + adaptability 25 + playoff translation 25. League ranks are used so raw ratings from different NBA scoring environments are not compared naively.

Resilience: each qualifying shock = preparedness 25 + replacement execution 25 + system adjustment 25 + result preservation 25.

What is the real counterargument?

Make resilience 30%, reduce the emphasis on postseason and development, and the Paul George core wins about 77.7 to 76.6. That is a coherent fan philosophy: value the longer regular-season contender and its ability to survive major injuries more than the newer core's Finals ceiling.

Random-weight results: 200,000 unrestricted models — Haliburton 80.248%, George 19.752%, O/S 0%. 100,000 bounded plausible models — Haliburton 91.62%, George 8.38%, O/S 0%. Fixed simulation seed: 42.

Limits and exclusions

What this report refuses to pretend it knows.

The Zubac trade is unresolved

The February 2026 trade is recorded, including the known premium asset cost, but excluded from the mature resource average because five Indiana games are not enough to grade the return.

2024–25 draft classes are immature

They remain tracked but are not forced into mature development scores before three NBA seasons exist.

Injuries are not front-office “mistakes”

The model grades preparation, replacement and adaptation—not moral responsibility for an injury occurring.

“No tank” is a qualified claim

Indiana had losing transition years and a 19–63 injury season. The claim is that the franchise repeatedly preserved star value rather than relying on a planned multi-year bottom-out as its primary path to the next centerpiece.

Core boundaries require judgment

Transition seasons are assigned by opening-season competitive identity. The 2021–22 season remains in the Sabonis core even though Haliburton arrived in February.

300,000 tests are not truth probability

They measure how often a ranking survives alternate priorities. They do not make a sports-history conclusion objectively 91.6% true.
