Indiana Pacers · 2010–2026

How did the Pacers keep turning one star into the next?

Indiana did not go directly from Paul George to Tyrese Haliburton. It preserved value through two different star exits, changed the style of basketball around the roster, and eventually reached Game 7 of the NBA Finals. The whole chain started with the No. 10 pick in 2010.

The quick answer

The Haliburton core wins. But the Paul George core has a real counterargument.

Haliburton produced the deepest run and the best combination of star succession, development, resource use and system fit. George's core was stronger across a longer regular-season sample and much better when its star disappeared.

Was the Paul George era actually great?

Yes. It was the strongest regular-season core—and the most resilient.

The Finals run makes the Haliburton era feel like the obvious winner in hindsight, but George's core had a stronger long-run floor. It reached consecutive Eastern Conference Finals, posted the best team-strength score in this study and remained nearly a playoff team even when George missed almost all of 2014–15.

Was the 2017 Paul George trade really a win?

It became far more valuable than the leverage suggested it should.

George had made his exit intention clear. Indiana still turned that forced-departure situation into two players who became All-Stars as Pacers. Oladipo gave Indiana an immediate star season. Sabonis became the durable branch of the return—and eventually the asset that brought Haliburton.

George → Oladipo + Sabonis94 / 100working conversion-event score

What this tells us: the trade did not just soften losing George. It created the material for the next two competitive cycles.

04

How much did Oladipo's injury change the chain?

It broke the first version of the new core before we ever saw its ceiling.

Oladipo's first Indiana season produced an All-Star, All-NBA, All-Defense and Most Improved Player peak. The next season Indiana again won 48 games—but his January 2019 quad tendon rupture ended the original trajectory. The roster could replace regular-season production better than it could replace playoff shot creation.

What survived

48 wins, elite defense, Sabonis' rise and another playoff berth.

What disappeared

The one-on-one star creation Indiana needed once the postseason compressed the game.

What this tells us: the Oladipo/Sabonis core should not be judged as if its planned lead star simply failed in the playoffs year after year. The core itself changed after the injury.

05

Why does Sabonis matter if that era never won a series?

Because the second branch of the George trade became the bridge to the Finals core.

Sabonis went from sixth man to two-time Pacers All-Star and the center of Indiana's offense. The core around him never broke through in the postseason, but his value did not evaporate when Indiana accepted the ceiling. It became the key outgoing asset in the Haliburton trade.

Zero series wins can describe a team's result without describing the value still sitting on the roster.

What this tells us: asset preservation is one reason Era Theory separates “how far did the team go?” from “what did the organization still have when the era ended?”

06

Why was the Sabonis-for-Haliburton trade different?

It did not just replace a star. It changed what kind of team Indiana could build.

Haliburton was younger, played a more scalable creation role and immediately gave Indiana an offensive organizing principle. The trade scored 99/100 in the conversion register. The next task was no longer “find another star.” It was “build a roster fast enough, skilled enough and deep enough to amplify this point guard.”

07

What did Carlisle and Haliburton actually change?

Indiana stopped asking one scorer to solve every possession and built a system around decisions.

The Haliburton core's best trait is synchronization. Carlisle's read-and-react offense, Haliburton's processing, Turner spacing, Nembhard's secondary creation and waves of runners/shooters made speed itself a roster feature. The system score is 94—the best of the three cores.

08

Why did Pascal Siakam complete the build?

Because the conversion chain finally produced overlapping star power.

Oladipo and Sabonis both became All-Stars, but their peaks never overlapped after Oladipo's injury. In 2024–25, Haliburton earned All-NBA while Siakam was an All-Star and then Eastern Conference Finals MVP. Indiana finally had two different high-end creators peaking inside the same deep run.

09

Why could the Finals roster replace almost everything except Haliburton?

The depth was real. The lead-creator dependency was real too.

Indiana lost backup centers James Wiseman and Isaiah Jackson to torn Achilles tendons in the first six games of 2024–25, patched the position and still reached Game 7 of the Finals. But during Haliburton's 10-game absence in 2022–23 the team went 1–9, and without him for all of 2025–26 Indiana fell to 19–63.

10

Did Indiana really avoid tanking?

Mostly—but “never had a bad season” would be the wrong story.

Indiana had losing transition seasons, including 25–57 in 2021–22 and the catastrophic 19–63 injury season in 2025–26. The evidence supports a narrower claim: the Pacers repeatedly preserved valuable players and trade assets rather than committing to a planned multi-year bottom-out as the primary route to the next centerpiece.

Keep useful value→Recognize the ceiling→Convert the centerpiece→Build around the new fit

What this tells us: the skill was conversion, not magic immunity from losing seasons.

Chapter 12 · Final answer

Indiana's defining skill was not rebuilding from nothing. It was refusing to let the previous era become nothing.

Paul George became Oladipo and Sabonis. Oladipo's branch was salvaged. Sabonis became Haliburton. Veteran assets became younger fits. Siakam was added when the new core was ready to accelerate. The Haliburton core is the best overall version because the chain finally produced the deepest run and the most synchronized organization.

Star-to-star conversion is the Pacers story: preserve enough value from the era that is ending to give the next era somewhere better than zero to begin.
