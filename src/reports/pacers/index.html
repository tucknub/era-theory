Indiana Pacers · 2010–2026

How did the Pacers keep turning one star into the next?

Indiana did not go directly from Paul George to Tyrese Haliburton. It preserved value through multiple star exits, changed the style around the roster, and eventually reached Game 7 of the NBA Finals.

The quick answer

The Haliburton core wins. The Paul George core has a real counterargument.

Haliburton owns the highest ceiling, best star conversion and strongest system fit. George owns the better regular-season floor, elite two-way peak concentration and resilience.

Was the George core actually great?

Yes—and it is the strongest alternative winner.

Indiana went 309–248, reached two Eastern Conference Finals and built back-to-back No. 1 defenses. When George missed almost all of 2014–15, Vogel's system still produced a 38–44 team with a top-10 defense.

Was the 2017 George trade really a win?

It became far more valuable than Indiana's leverage suggested.

The forced exit produced Victor Oladipo and Domantas Sabonis. Oladipo immediately became All-NBA, All-Defense and Most Improved. Sabonis later became a two-time Pacers All-Star—and the centerpiece of the Haliburton trade.

What this tells us: the George trade did not merely replace one player. It supplied material for two future cores.

Why did the Oladipo/Sabonis core stall?

Its elite peaks never fully overlapped.

Oladipo's 2017–18 season was genuinely elite. His 2019 quad rupture broke that version of the team. Sabonis rose later, Turner remained a high-level specialist, and Indiana kept winning regular-season games—but the core never won a playoff series.

Why was Sabonis-for-Haliburton different?

Indiana traded a good All-Star for a younger engine that changed how the entire team played.

The transition grades 98.5 because it preserved outgoing value, solved the next lead-star question and eventually raised the ceiling to the NBA Finals.

What this tells us: star succession became an organizational multiplier instead of a reset button.

06

What did Carlisle and Haliburton change?

The roster finally had an identity that matched its best player.

Indiana became 4th in pace in 2022–23, then 2nd in pace and offensive rating in 2023–24. By 2024–25 the offense remained top-10 while the defense improved to the middle of the league.

Why did Siakam complete the build?

He gave the fast system a second way to win.

Indiana paid a premium price, then retained Siakam long term. He added a veteran half-court scorer and became the 2025 Eastern Conference Finals MVP.

What this tells us: the best transaction was not always the cheapest one. Fit and retained value mattered.

08

How dependent is the modern core on Haliburton?

More than the Finals run makes it feel.

Indiana was 7–19 without Haliburton in 2022–23. In 2023–24, Nembhard, McConnell and Siakam helped the team reach the ECF through injuries. But when Haliburton missed all of 2025–26, the offense fell to 29th and the Pacers went 19–63.

09

Did Indiana really avoid tanking?

It avoided rebuilding from zero. That is the more accurate claim.

Indiana absolutely had bad seasons: 25–57 in 2021–22, 35–47 in 2022–23 and the injury-driven 19–63 season in 2025–26. What it did not do between George and Haliburton was deliberately spend years liquidating everything for repeated top-five lottery chances.

“No long tank” does not mean “never lost.” It means the next core was repeatedly financed by value already inside the organization.

Chapter 12 · Final answer

Indiana's repeatable advantage was continuity of value.

The Haliburton core is the best overall core in the published model because it turned the conversion chain into the highest postseason ceiling. The George core remains a legitimate alternative if you value sustained floor and two-way resilience more. The middle core matters because it kept the asset chain alive.

The Pacers did not keep finding a brand-new franchise. They kept turning the value of the old one into the next one.
