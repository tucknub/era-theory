Indiana Pacers · 2010–2026

How did the Pacers keep turning one star into the next?

Paul George became Victor Oladipo and Domantas Sabonis. Sabonis became Tyrese Haliburton. Haliburton became the engine of a 2025 NBA Finals team. The whole chain started with the No. 10 pick in 2010.

The quick answer

The Haliburton core wins. The Paul George core has a real counterargument.

Haliburton produced the deepest run and the best combination of succession, development, resource use and system fit. George was stronger over a longer regular-season sample and far more resilient.

Was the George era actually great?

Yes. It was the strongest regular-season core—and the most resilient.

Two Eastern Conference Finals, the best team-strength score in the study, and a defense strong enough to keep Indiana near the playoffs when George missed almost all of 2014–15.

03

Was the 2017 trade really a win?

It became more valuable than the leverage suggested it should.

George's exit returned two players who became Pacers All-Stars. Oladipo supplied the immediate peak; Sabonis became the durable branch and ultimately the asset that brought Haliburton.

It did not merely soften losing George. It created the material for the next two competitive cycles.
04

How much did Oladipo's injury change?

It broke the first version of the new core before we saw its ceiling.

His first Indiana season produced All-Star, All-NBA, All-Defense and Most Improved Player honors. The 2019 quad tendon rupture changed the hierarchy. Indiana could replace regular-season production better than playoff shot creation.

05

Why does Sabonis matter with zero series wins?

Because the second branch of the George trade became the bridge to the Finals core.

Sabonis became a two-time Pacers All-Star and the center of the offense. When Indiana accepted the ceiling, his value did not evaporate. It became Tyrese Haliburton.

06

Why was the Haliburton trade different?

It changed what kind of team Indiana could build.

Haliburton was younger and played a more scalable creation role. The next question became how to surround his processing with speed, spacing, secondary creators and enough defense.

2023–24

No. 2 offense, No. 2 pace, Eastern Conference Finals.

2024–25

More balance, 50 wins, NBA Finals Game 7.

07

What did Carlisle and Haliburton change?

The roster stopped asking one scorer to solve every possession and started amplifying decisions.

Transition pressure, fast reads, Turner spacing, Nembhard secondary creation and a deep running/shooting rotation created the best roster-system synchronization in the study: 94.0.

08

Why did Siakam complete the build?

Because the conversion chain finally produced overlapping star power.

Haliburton earned All-NBA while Siakam became a Pacers All-Star and then 2025 Eastern Conference Finals MVP. The previous Oladipo/Sabonis core had two All-Star branches, but their peaks never overlapped after Oladipo's injury.

09

Why could the Finals roster replace almost everything except Haliburton?

The depth was real. The lead-creator dependency was real too.

Indiana lost both backup centers to Achilles tears in the first six games of 2024–25, patched the position and reached Finals Game 7. But it went 1–9 during Haliburton's 2022–23 absence and 19–63 without him in 2025–26.

Best response

Center crisis, 2024–25: 50 wins and Finals.

Structural weakness

Full Haliburton absence, 2025–26: 19–63.

Chapter 12 · Final answer

Indiana's defining skill was not rebuilding from nothing. It was refusing to let the previous era become nothing.

Paul George became Oladipo and Sabonis. Sabonis became Haliburton. Veteran assets became younger fits. Siakam arrived when the new core was ready to accelerate. The Haliburton core wins because the conversion chain finally produced the deepest run and the most synchronized organization.

Star-to-star conversion: preserve enough value from the era that is ending to give the next era somewhere better than zero to begin.
